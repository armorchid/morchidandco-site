Spouse, partner and family visas

How much do I need to earn to sponsor my spouse to come to the UK?

The minimum income requirement for a partner visa has been £29,000 a year since 11 April 2024. It can be met from the UK sponsor's salary, the applicant's UK job offer in some cases, certain savings, pensions or a combination. Some sponsors, for example those receiving certain disability or carer benefits, are exempt and must instead show adequate maintenance. The government has said it is reviewing the family rules, so check the current figure before you apply.

Can I use savings instead of income for a spouse visa?

Yes. Cash savings can be used alone or with income. To rely on savings alone you need at least £88,500 held for six months. If your income is below £29,000, the shortfall is multiplied by 2.5 and added to £16,000 to give the savings you need. The money must be in your or your partner's name and properly evidenced with bank statements.

What English level does my partner need?

For a first partner visa the applicant usually needs English at CEFR level A1 (speaking and listening) from an approved test provider, unless exempt by nationality, a degree taught in English, age or disability. A higher level, A2, is needed for the extension, and a higher level again for settlement. Requirements are being tightened as part of the settlement reforms, so we check which level applies at the time you apply.

How long does a spouse visa last, and when can my partner settle?

A partner visa is normally granted for 33 months from outside the UK and 30 months when extending inside the UK. After two grants, totalling five years, your partner can usually apply for indefinite leave to remain. Under the government's earned settlement plans, partners of British citizens are expected to keep a five-year route, but the detailed rules have not yet been published.

Can my fiancé(e) come to the UK to marry me?

Yes. A fiancé(e) or proposed civil partner visa lets your partner come to the UK for six months to marry or form a civil partnership, then switch to a partner visa without leaving the UK. The same financial, English and accommodation requirements apply as for a spouse visa. Your partner cannot work during the six months.

We are not married. Can my partner still get a visa?

Yes, if you have been living together in a relationship similar to marriage for at least two years before the application. You will need evidence of living together at the same address, such as tenancy agreements, bills and bank statements in both names, spread across the two years.

Can I bring my children to the UK?

Children under 18 can usually join a parent who is in the UK, or applying at the same time, on a family or work route. The rules depend on the parent's visa, whether both parents are coming, and who has sole responsibility for the child. Extra income is needed for each child on a partner route.

Can I bring my elderly parents to live with me in the UK?

Only in limited cases, through the adult dependent relative route. You must show your parent needs long-term personal care because of age, illness or disability, and that the care is not available or affordable in their country. The route is strict and refusals are common, so advice before applying is strongly recommended.

Can I stay in the UK if my relationship breaks down?

Your partner visa depends on the relationship, so if it ends the Home Office may curtail your permission. You may have other options, such as a different visa, a claim based on your children or private life, or the domestic abuse provisions if the relationship ended because of abuse. Get advice quickly, as deadlines can apply.

What if my partner visa application is refused?

Partner refusals usually carry a right of appeal on human rights grounds. You normally have 14 days to appeal if you are in the UK and 28 days if you are outside. Sometimes a fresh, stronger application is quicker than an appeal. Send us the full refusal letter and we will explain which option is best.

Visit visas and the ETA

Do I need a visa or an ETA to visit the UK?

It depends on your nationality. Visa nationals, including nationals of Morocco, Algeria, Egypt and many other countries, need a visit visa before travelling. Most non-visa nationals, including EU, US and Canadian citizens, now need an Electronic Travel Authorisation (ETA). Since 25 February 2026 airlines must refuse boarding to travellers who need an ETA and do not have one. British and Irish citizens do not need either.

How long can I stay on a UK visit visa?

A standard visit visa normally lets you stay for up to six months per visit. You can apply for a long-term visit visa valid for 2, 5 or 10 years, but each stay is still normally limited to six months. You must show you are a genuine visitor who will leave at the end of each visit.

Why are visit visas refused?

The most common reasons are that the Home Office is not satisfied you will leave the UK, that your funds are not clearly explained, or that the purpose of the visit is unclear. Large unexplained deposits, gaps in employment evidence and inconsistent details are frequent problems. We help you show your ties at home, your finances and your plans clearly.

Can I appeal a visit visa refusal?

There is generally no right of appeal against a visit visa refusal, unless the decision refuses a human rights claim. The usual route is a fresh application that deals with every reason given for the refusal. A judicial review is possible in limited cases where the decision is unlawful.

Can I work or study on a visit visa?

You cannot work for a UK employer or do paid work in the UK on a visit visa. Some business activities, such as attending meetings, conferences and negotiating deals, are allowed. You can do a short course of study of up to six months. You cannot switch from a visit visa to most other visas from inside the UK.

How long is an ETA valid?

An ETA is usually valid for two years, or until your passport expires if sooner, and allows multiple visits of up to six months each. It is linked digitally to your passport, so you need a new ETA if you get a new passport. An ETA is permission to travel, not a guarantee of entry.

Work visas and sponsor licences

What are the requirements for a Skilled Worker visa?

You need a job offer from a Home Office licensed sponsor, a Certificate of Sponsorship, a job at the required skill level (degree level, RQF 6, for most new applicants since July 2025), a salary that meets the relevant threshold, and English at level B2 for new applicants since 8 January 2026. Workers already on the route when the rules changed may benefit from transitional arrangements.

What is the minimum salary for a Skilled Worker visa?

Since 22 July 2025 the general threshold has been £41,700 a year, or the going rate for the occupation if higher, with lower rates for some new entrants and certain roles. The government announced in the 2026 Autumn Budget that occupation thresholds will move to a different calculation from spring 2027. We check the exact figure for the job before you commit.

Can care workers still come to the UK?

Overseas recruitment of care workers and senior care workers closed on 22 July 2025. People already in the UK in the care sector can, for a transitional period, extend or switch within the route, and some care workers who entered before the May 2025 White Paper are expected to keep a five-year settlement route. Get advice on your exact position.

How do I get a sponsor licence for my business?

You apply online to the Home Office with evidence that your business is genuine and operating in the UK, nominate key people to manage the licence, and show you have suitable HR systems. The Home Office may visit before deciding. Standard processing takes around eight weeks. A well-prepared application avoids delays and refusals.

What are a sponsor's main duties?

Sponsors must check and keep records of right to work, monitor sponsored workers' attendance and pay, report certain changes to the Home Office through the sponsor management system within the deadlines, and cooperate with compliance visits. The Home Office tightened its sponsor guidance in March 2026 and can revoke a licence even where breaches are unintentional.

Can a sponsor licence be revoked, and what happens to workers?

Yes. The Home Office can suspend, downgrade or revoke a licence for breaches of sponsor duties. If a licence is revoked, sponsored workers' permission is usually cut short, and they normally have a limited period to find a new sponsor, switch visa or leave. The 2026 Autumn Budget announced more time for some highly skilled workers to find a new job from 2027.

Can I change employer on a Skilled Worker visa?

Yes, but you must make a new Skilled Worker application with a Certificate of Sponsorship from the new licensed sponsor before you start the new job. You can usually start work once the application is made, provided it was made before your current permission expires.

Can I bring my family on a work visa?

Skilled Worker visa holders can usually bring a partner and children under 18 as dependants. Overseas care workers recruited after March 2024 cannot bring dependants. Dependants pay their own application fee and Immigration Health Surcharge.

Students and graduates

How much money do I need for a UK student visa?

You must show your course fees for the first year plus living costs for up to nine months. From 30 November 2026 the living cost figure is £1,570 a month in London and £1,203 a month elsewhere. The money must usually be held for 28 consecutive days before you apply, unless you are exempt.

Can I bring my family on a student visa?

Since January 2024 most students cannot bring dependants. The exception is students on postgraduate research courses, such as a PhD, and some government-sponsored students.

How long is the Graduate visa?

The Graduate visa currently gives two years (three years for PhD graduates) to live and work in the UK after a degree. For applications made from 1 January 2027 it is reduced to 18 months for most graduates, with PhD graduates keeping three years. Time on the Graduate route is not expected to count towards settlement under the government's plans.

Can I switch from a student visa to a work visa?

Yes, you can usually switch to the Skilled Worker route from inside the UK once you meet its requirements. There are limits on switching before your course has finished. You can also switch to the Innovator Founder route after completing your course.

ILR, settlement and British citizenship

What is earned settlement, and does it affect me?

Earned settlement is the government's plan, confirmed on 2 November 2026, to make 10 years the standard qualifying period for indefinite leave to remain, with shorter routes for higher earners and certain groups, and longer routes for people with immigration breaches or recent use of public funds. It is policy, not yet law: the Immigration Rules are expected to change from December 2026, with implementation in 2027. Book a Settlement Check to see how it may affect you.

How long do I need to live in the UK to get ILR?

Under the current rules most work and family routes lead to ILR after five years. Some routes, such as Global Talent, can be shorter, and the long residence route currently requires 10 years of continuous lawful residence. These periods are expected to change under the earned settlement reforms, so check before you plan.

Who keeps a five-year route under the new plans?

According to the government's November 2026 response, groups expected to keep a five-year route include eligible partners and children of British citizens, EU Settlement Scheme pre-settled status holders, UK Ancestry and Hong Kong BN(O) visa holders, and some doctors, nurses and teachers. Strengthened minimum requirements are still expected to apply. Details will be in the new rules.

Is the 10-year long residence route closing?

The government has said it will close the long residence route and that time on temporary routes such as Student and Graduate visas will no longer count towards settlement. Until the new rules take effect the current route remains open. If you are close to 10 years, speak to us soon.

What is the Life in the UK test?

It is a computer-based test of 24 questions about British history, traditions and everyday life, which most applicants aged 18 to 64 must pass for ILR and citizenship. You book it online and take it at an approved test centre. You need 18 correct answers to pass.

How long after ILR can I apply for British citizenship?

Usually 12 months after getting ILR, and after at least five years' residence in the UK. If you are married to or in a civil partnership with a British citizen, you can apply as soon as you have ILR, after three years' residence. You must also meet the absence limits, English, Life in the UK and good character requirements.

How many days can I spend outside the UK for citizenship?

For naturalisation after five years you should normally have spent no more than 450 days outside the UK in the five years before applying, and no more than 90 days in the last 12 months. The Home Office has some discretion to overlook excess absences in certain circumstances.

What is the good character requirement?

Citizenship applicants must show good character. Criminal convictions, immigration breaches, unpaid tax or debts, and dishonesty in past applications can all count against you. Since February 2025, people who entered the UK illegally, including by small boat, are normally refused citizenship regardless of how long ago they arrived.

Can my child born in the UK become British?

A child born in the UK is automatically British if, at the time of birth, either parent was British or settled (for example, had ILR). Otherwise, the child may be able to register as British later, for example once a parent gets ILR or citizenship, or after living in the UK for the first 10 years of life.

How much does it cost to apply for ILR or citizenship?

Home Office fees increased on 8 April 2026. The ILR application fee is now £3,226 and naturalisation is £1,709, plus the citizenship ceremony fee. These fees are paid to the Home Office and are separate from our professional fees.

Refusals, reviews and appeals

My visa was refused. What are my options?

Depending on the type of decision, you may be able to ask for an administrative review, appeal to the First-tier Tribunal, make a fresh application or, in limited cases, apply for judicial review. The refusal letter tells you which route applies. Deadlines are short, so contact us as soon as you receive it.

What is an administrative review?

An administrative review asks the Home Office to check whether a caseworking error was made, for example in points-based or Skilled Worker refusals. You usually have 14 days to apply if you are in the UK, 28 days if you are outside, and 7 days if you are detained. It is not a chance to submit new evidence in most cases. Administrative review for EU Settlement Scheme decisions was removed from 8 October 2026.

How long do I have to appeal to the tribunal?

Usually 14 days from the date the decision was sent if you are in the UK, and 28 days if you are outside the UK. Shorter deadlines apply if you are detained. A late appeal can sometimes be accepted with a good reason, but you should never rely on that.

How long do immigration appeals take?

It varies by tribunal centre and case type. Many First-tier Tribunal appeals take several months from lodging to hearing. If the tribunal refuses your appeal, you may be able to ask for permission to appeal to the Upper Tribunal on a point of law.

Should I appeal or reapply?

If the refusal was caused by missing or weak evidence that you can now provide, a fresh application may be faster and cheaper. If the decision is wrong in law or fails to consider your human rights, an appeal may be better. We review the refusal letter and recommend the most realistic option.

Can I stay in the UK while my appeal is pending?

If you applied before your previous permission expired and appealed in time, you are usually treated as having permission on the same conditions while the appeal is pending. This is known as section 3C leave. Your right to work and other conditions usually continue.

What happens if I overstay my visa?

Overstaying is a breach of immigration law. It can lead to removal, refusal of future applications and a re-entry ban of up to 10 years, depending on how long you overstayed and how you leave. Under the earned settlement plans, adult immigration breaches such as overstaying would also put people on a much longer route to settlement. Get advice before your permission runs out.

Asylum and protection

How do I claim asylum in the UK?

You should claim asylum as soon as possible after arriving, ideally at the port of entry. If you are already in the UK, you register your claim with the Home Office, which will arrange a screening appointment and later a substantive interview. Claiming late can damage your credibility, so get advice quickly.

How long is refugee status granted for now?

For adults claiming asylum from 2 March 2026, refugee status is granted for 30 months and reviewed before it ends. Protection is renewed if it is still needed. The government has also said refugees on this core protection route will have a much longer wait for settlement unless they move to a work or study route.

Can asylum seekers work in the UK?

Usually not. You can apply for permission to work if your claim has been waiting for more than 12 months through no fault of your own, and then only in jobs on the Immigration Salary List. Asylum support may be available if you would otherwise be destitute.

Can refugees bring their family to the UK?

The refugee family reunion route was suspended for new applications from 4 September 2025. Refugees can still apply under the standard family rules, which include a minimum income requirement. Children and other vulnerable family members may have other options. Ask us about your situation.

What is a fresh claim?

If your asylum or human rights claim has been refused and any appeal has ended, you can make further submissions with new evidence. The Home Office decides whether they amount to a fresh claim with a realistic prospect of success. If accepted and refused, you may get a new right of appeal.

EU citizens and the EU Settlement Scheme

Can I still apply to the EU Settlement Scheme?

The main deadline passed on 30 June 2021, but late applications are accepted where you have reasonable grounds for missing it. Joining family members and people with pre-settled status can still apply in some circumstances.

Will my pre-settled status become settled status automatically?

The Home Office has started converting eligible pre-settled status holders to settled status automatically, using tax and benefit records to confirm continuous residence. If your status has not been converted and you think you qualify, you can still apply. Check your UKVI account.

Do EU citizens need an ETA to visit the UK?

Yes. EU citizens visiting the UK without UK immigration status need an ETA. If you have EU Settlement Scheme status, you do not need an ETA, but your passport must be linked to your UKVI account.

eVisas and practical questions

What is an eVisa and how do I get one?

An eVisa is a digital record of your immigration status, held in your UKVI account. Physical BRP cards have been replaced, and the Home Office stopped putting visa stickers in passports for new grants from 1 July 2026. Create a UKVI account, link your current passport, and keep your details up to date.

How do I prove my right to work or rent?

Use the Home Office online services to generate a share code from your UKVI account and give it to your employer or landlord, who checks it online. British and Irish citizens can use their passport instead.

I have a new passport. What do I need to do?

Update your passport details in your UKVI account before you travel. If you hold an ETA, you need a new ETA for the new passport. Travelling on a passport that is not linked can cause delays or refusal of boarding.

How long does the Home Office take to decide applications?

Published service standards are around three weeks for most visas applied for outside the UK and eight weeks for most applications inside the UK, but times vary and complex cases take longer. Priority and super priority services are available for some routes for an extra fee.

What is the Immigration Health Surcharge?

It is a fee most visa applicants pay to use the NHS. It is charged per year of the visa and paid upfront with the application. Some applicants, such as health and care workers on certain routes, are exempt or can claim a refund.
