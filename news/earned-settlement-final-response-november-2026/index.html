Earned settlement confirmed: 10 years becomes the standard route to ILR

The government has confirmed its earned settlement model. Most people will wait 10 years for indefinite leave to remain, with shorter routes for higher earners and some key groups.

Event date: 2 November 2026By: Mr A. Morchid, IAA Level 3 adviser

On 2 November 2026 the Home Secretary set out the government's final response to the earned settlement consultation. The standard qualifying period for indefinite leave to remain (ILR) will rise from five to 10 years for most people, including many who are already in the UK and have not yet settled.

Shorter and longer routes

  • 3 years for people earning at least £125,141 a year in three of the previous four years.
  • 5 years for people earning at least £50,271 a year in three of the previous five years.
  • 10 years for people earning at least £12,571 a year in five of the previous seven years.
  • 20 years for adults with specified immigration breaches, such as overstaying, or recent use of specified public funds.

Couples can combine earnings to meet a £25,142 household minimum. Applicants are expected to need English at B2 level and to pass the Life in the UK test, subject to exemptions, and an adult UK criminal record will lead to refusal.

Who keeps five years

Groups expected to keep a five-year route include eligible partners and children of British citizens, EU Settlement Scheme pre-settled status holders, UK Ancestry and Hong Kong BN(O) visa holders, and some doctors, nurses and teachers. Some care workers who arrived before the May 2025 White Paper also keep five years.

The long residence route will close

The 10-year long residence route will close, and time on Student, Graduate and some other temporary routes will no longer count towards settlement.

When it starts

This is policy, not yet law. Changes to the Immigration Rules are expected from December 2026, with implementation during 2027. Start dates and the treatment of pending applications are not yet confirmed.
