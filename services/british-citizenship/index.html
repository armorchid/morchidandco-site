British citizenship: naturalisation and registration

Becoming British is the final step for many of our clients. We check your eligibility carefully, including absences and good character, and prepare your application so it is right first time.

Message us on WhatsAppSee our fees

Naturalisation requirements

  • Settled status: indefinite leave to remain or EU settled status, usually held for 12 months (not needed if married to a British citizen).
  • Residence: five years in the UK (three years if married to or in a civil partnership with a British citizen).
  • Absences: normally no more than 450 days outside the UK in the five years before applying, and no more than 90 days in the last 12 months. The Home Office has some discretion.
  • English at B1 or above, or an exemption, and the Life in the UK test.
  • Good character: criminal convictions, immigration breaches, unpaid tax or debts and past dishonesty can lead to refusal. Since February 2025, people who entered the UK illegally are normally refused regardless of how long ago they arrived.
  • Intention to live in the UK and two referees.

Registering a child as British

A child born in the UK is automatically British if, at birth, a parent was British or settled. Otherwise, the child may be able to register, for example once a parent becomes settled or British, or after living in the UK for the first 10 years of life. Children born abroad to British parents may also qualify. The Home Office fee for registering a child is £1,000 from 8 April 2026.

Frequently asked questions

How long after ILR can I apply for citizenship?

Usually after holding ILR for 12 months, with five years' residence. If married to a British citizen, you can apply as soon as you have ILR.

What if I have been outside the UK for more than 450 days?

The Home Office can sometimes overlook excess absences, depending on how many days and why. We assess this before you apply.

Will a driving offence stop me becoming British?

It may. Convictions and some out-of-court disposals affect good character. Tell us about any offence so we can advise before you apply.
