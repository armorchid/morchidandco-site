Visa refused? Reviews, appeals and fresh applications

Act quickly: refusal deadlines

Deadlines to challenge a Home Office decision are short and strictly applied:

  • Administrative review: usually 14 days if you are in the UK, 28 days if outside, 7 days if detained.
  • Appeal to the First-tier Tribunal: usually 14 days from the date the decision was sent if you are in the UK, 28 days if outside the UK, and shorter if you are detained.
  • Judicial review: promptly, and in any event within three months, where there is no right of appeal.

Which challenge applies to your refusal?

Appeal to the tribunal

A right of appeal usually exists where the Home Office refuses a human rights claim (for example most spouse, partner, parent, private life and long residence refusals), a protection (asylum) claim, or an EU Settlement Scheme application. The appeal is heard by an independent judge at the First-tier Tribunal (Immigration and Asylum Chamber). If the judge gets the law wrong, you may seek permission to appeal to the Upper Tribunal.

Administrative review

Administrative review applies to many points-based refusals, such as Skilled Worker and Student visas. It asks the Home Office to correct a caseworking error. New evidence is generally not allowed. Administrative review for EU Settlement Scheme decisions was removed on 8 October 2026.

Fresh application

If the refusal was caused by missing or weak evidence, a new, stronger application is often quicker and cheaper than an appeal. Visit visa refusals normally have no right of appeal, so a well-prepared fresh application is usually the answer.

Judicial review

Where there is no appeal or review, an unlawful decision can be challenged by judicial review in the Upper Tribunal. We tell you if your case needs a solicitor or barrister for this stage.

How an immigration appeal works

  1. Review: we assess the refusal and your chances honestly, including when an appeal is unlikely to succeed.
  2. Lodge the appeal online before the deadline, with grounds of appeal.
  3. Prepare the bundle: witness statements, evidence and a skeleton argument.
  4. Hearing: representation at the First-tier Tribunal, often by video.
  5. Decision and next steps: we explain the outcome and any further options.

If you applied in time before your visa expired, you can usually stay in the UK on the same conditions while your appeal is pending (section 3C leave).

Frequently asked questions

Can I appeal a UK visa refusal?

It depends on the decision. Human rights, protection and EU Settlement Scheme refusals usually carry a right of appeal. Points-based refusals usually go to administrative review. Visit visa refusals usually have no appeal.

How long does an immigration appeal take?

Many First-tier Tribunal appeals take several months from lodging to hearing, depending on the tribunal centre.

Is it better to appeal or reapply?

If the refusal was caused by missing evidence, reapplying is often faster. If the decision is wrong in law or ignores your human rights, an appeal may be better. We advise after reading the refusal letter.

Can I work while my appeal is pending?

If you applied before your permission expired and appealed in time, your existing conditions, including the right to work, usually continue.
