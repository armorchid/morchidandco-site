Who a UK spouse visa is for

The UK partner route, often called the spouse visa or family visa, is for the husband, wife, civil partner, fiancé(e) or unmarried partner of a British citizen, a person with settled status (such as indefinite leave to remain), or someone with refugee status or humanitarian protection. Unmarried partners usually need to have lived together for at least two years.

We help with every stage of the route:

  • Spouse visa from outside the UK (entry clearance), for example from Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia, Egypt, Pakistan, Nigeria or anywhere else.
  • Fiancé(e) visa, to come to the UK to marry within six months and then switch to a spouse visa.
  • Spouse visa extension (FLR(M)) inside the UK after the first 33 or 30 months.
  • Indefinite leave to remain as a partner after five years.
  • Spouse visa refusals, appeals and fresh applications.

Spouse visa requirements in 2026

1. Financial requirement: £29,000

The minimum income requirement has been £29,000 a year since 11 April 2024. It can be met from the UK partner's employment or self-employment, the applicant's job in the UK in some cases, pensions, certain non-employment income, cash savings, or a combination. To rely on savings alone you need £88,500 held for at least six months. If income is below £29,000, the shortfall is multiplied by 2.5 and added to £16,000 to give the savings needed. Sponsors receiving certain disability or carer benefits are exempt and must instead show adequate maintenance. The government has said it is reviewing the family rules, so we always confirm the figure in force when you apply.

2. Genuine and subsisting relationship

You must show your relationship is genuine and that you intend to live together permanently in the UK. Useful evidence includes your marriage certificate, photographs together over time, travel records, call and message history, and proof of living together or visiting each other.

3. English language

The applicant usually needs English at CEFR level A1 in speaking and listening for the first visa, from an approved Secure English Language Test provider, unless exempt by nationality, an English-taught degree, age or disability. Level A2 is needed for the extension and a higher level for settlement.

4. Adequate accommodation

You must have accommodation that you own or occupy exclusively, without overcrowding and without claiming public funds. A tenancy agreement or mortgage statement and, where relevant, a property inspection report or letter from the owner, are usually needed.

5. Suitability and other requirements

The Home Office also checks criminality, immigration history and previous applications. Applicants from some countries need a tuberculosis (TB) test certificate.

Documents checklist

  • Passports for both partners, and the UK partner's proof of British citizenship or settled status
  • Marriage or civil partnership certificate, or evidence of two years living together
  • Payslips, bank statements, employer letter and P60 for the specified period (or self-employment evidence)
  • English language test certificate or proof of exemption
  • Tenancy agreement or mortgage statement, and accommodation evidence
  • Relationship evidence: photos, travel, communication
  • Certified translations of any document not in English or Welsh

We send you a tailored checklist after your consultation, because the right evidence depends on your situation.

Spouse visa fees and processing times

From 8 April 2026 the Home Office fee is £2,064 for a spouse visa from outside the UK and £1,407 for an extension inside the UK, plus the Immigration Health Surcharge for each year of the visa. Our professional fee is a fixed £800 for a spouse, partner or fiancé(e) visa from abroad and £700 for an extension. Home Office decisions on family visas from outside the UK usually take several weeks to a few months, depending on the country; priority services are available in some locations.

Why spouse visas are refused

  • Income or savings evidence that does not cover the exact period or format the rules require
  • Gaps or inconsistencies in relationship evidence
  • Wrong English test or an expired certificate
  • Accommodation evidence missing or showing overcrowding
  • Previous immigration breaches not properly explained

Most refusals are avoidable with careful preparation. If you have already been refused, see our visa refusal and appeals service.

Frequently asked questions

How much income do I need for a UK spouse visa?

£29,000 a year under the current rules, from employment, self-employment, pensions, other income or savings. Savings alone must be at least £88,500 held for six months.

How long does a spouse visa take?

Decisions on spouse visas from outside the UK usually take several weeks to a few months, depending on the visa application centre. Extensions inside the UK usually take around eight weeks.

Can my spouse work in the UK?

Yes. A partner visa allows your spouse to work and study in the UK without restriction, but not to claim most public funds.

Can I apply for a spouse visa from a visit visa?

Generally no. Partners usually cannot switch from a visit visa to a spouse visa inside the UK, except as a fiancé(e) who has married in the UK. We check whether any exception applies to you.

When can my spouse get indefinite leave to remain?

Usually after five years on the partner route. Under the government's earned settlement plans, partners of British citizens are expected to keep a five-year route.
